Behind the Badge

Exploring NYPD Civilian Complaint Outcomes

Jiamin Wu · Justin Chanthabandith · Matthew Takeuchi · Kristen Oleson

Every year, thousands of New Yorkers file complaints against NYPD officers with the Civilian Complaint Review Board (CCRB). But what actually happens after a complaint is filed?

Using ProPublica's dataset of 33,358 allegations from about 12,000 complaints (1985–2020), we found that only about 1 in 4 allegations is substantiated. Almost half are closed as unsubstantiated, meaning the board could not find enough evidence either way. Does the outcome change with the type of complaint and the people involved?

Prototype Write-up

What have we done so far?

So far, we chose ProPublica's NYPD civilian complaints dataset (about 33,000 allegations from 1985–2020) and made six static charts in a Python notebook to explore it. These covered misconduct types, resolution times by complainant–officer ethnicity and gender, findings by officer rank, and outcomes by the reason for police contact. Based on what we found, we decided on our main feature: readers build a hypothetical complaint and see how similar past cases turned out. We then built this first D3 version of the tool, where six dropdowns (misconduct type, reason for contact, and complainant and officer ethnicity and gender) filter the data and update a bar chart of outcomes, compared against all allegations. The page also shows how many cases each result is based on and the median time to close, and it is published on GitHub Pages.

What will be the most challenging part to design, and why?

The hardest design problem is small sample sizes: when readers combine several filters, the number of matching cases can drop to just a few, and the percentages become misleading. Right now we only show a warning below 30 cases, but we want a clearer way to show that uncertainty in the chart itself without cluttering it. Another challenge is wording, because outcomes like "unsubstantiated" and "exonerated" have specific meanings that many readers misunderstand, so our annotations need to explain them in plain language. We also still need to design the story around the tool (a hook, guided examples, and takeaways) and possibly a flow chart of how a complaint moves through the system. Finally, this is a sensitive topic, so we want the design to stay neutral and not suggest conclusions the data can't support.

Data: ProPublica, Civilian Complaints Against New York City Police Officers (CCRB records of closed allegations, 1985–2020). Each row is one allegation against one officer.
